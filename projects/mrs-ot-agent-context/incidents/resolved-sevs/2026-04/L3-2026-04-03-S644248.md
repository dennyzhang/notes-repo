## Overview
On April 2, 2026, at approximately 9:45 pm PDT, the IG Reels retrieval Omni Root model’s online training pipeline began experiencing severe performance degradation, with its training QPS (queries per second) frequently dropping to zero. This issue persisted for over 10 hours, until it was detected at 8:00 am PDT the following day and subsequently diagnosed and mitigated within the next four hours. The incident directly impacted the quality of Instagram Reels recommendations, resulting in worse recs contents for users consuming IG Reels, though no revenue impact was explicitly noted.
Root Cause and Technical Breakdown
The SEV was triggered by a misconfiguration in the model’s multicast subscription settings. Specifically, the production Omni Root model (ID: 2137792444) was subscribed to 14 ST models via multicast, a legacy from its previous role as a prod refresh arm. This excessive number of listeners overwhelmed system resources, causing the training pipeline to be blocked by snapshot registration and sparse delta publishing activities, rather than actual training tasks. Logs confirmed the absence of training activity and highlighted resource contention. The root cause was traced to the MRS_DELTA_UPDATE_SUBSCRIBER sitevar configuration, which was not properly pruned during the model’s transition to its new role.
Incident Response and Remediation
Upon detection, the team quickly escalated the issue in the workchat thread (https://chat.google.com/room/AAQA0dSMWtI), engaging model owners and infrastructure experts. Remediation involved auditing and reducing multicast subscriptions, executed through D99462463 (https://www.internalfb.com/diff/D99462463) and D99470486 (https://www.internalfb.com/diff/D99470486), which updated the sitevar configuration and removed unnecessary models. These actions restored training QPS and resolved the incident. Going forward, the team has committed to collaborative review and stamping of multicast sitevar changes to prevent unchecked subscription growth and ensure configuration hygiene. This SEV underscores the importance of operational safeguards and proactive monitoring for critical model infrastructure.

## Root cause

The root cause was traced to the production Omni model (ID: 2137792444), which had been subscribed to 14 ST models via multicast. This over-subscription led to the online training QPS (queries per second) frequently dropping to zero, severely impacting model training and resulting in degraded recommendations content.
Technical Breakdown
• Multicast Over-Subscription: The Omni Root model was configured to multicast updates to too many subscriber models. This saturation caused the training process to be blocked, as system resources were overwhelmed by snapshot registration and sparse delta publishing activities, rather than actual training tasks.
• Configuration Drift: The model had previously served as a prod refresh arm, accumulating numerous ST model listeners. When it transitioned to the new prod model, these subscriptions were not pruned, leading to resource contention.
• Directly Involved Components: The issue was rooted in the MRS_DELTA_UPDATE_SUBSCRIBER (https://www.internalfb.com/diff/D99462463) sitevar configuration, which governs multicast subscriptions. The excessive listeners caused the training QPS to drop, as confirmed by logs showing no training activity but heavy snapshot registration.
5 Whys Analysis
Why did QPS drop to zero? Because the training pipeline was blocked by excessive multicast activity.
Why was there excessive multicast activity? The prod Omni model was subscribed to too many ST models.
Why were there so many subscriptions? Historical configuration from its previous role as a prod refresh arm was not cleaned up.
Why was cleanup not performed? There was no enforced process for pruning subscriptions during model role transitions.
Why is there no process? Lack of clear guidance and review for multicast subscription changes, leading to unchecked growth.
In summary, the SEV was caused by unchecked multicast subscription growth in the Omni Root model’s configuration, resulting in resource exhaustion and blocked online training.

## Remediation
To address the SEV impacting the IG Reels retrieval Omni Root model’s online training QPS, immediate remediation actions were taken to restore system stability and model performance. The primary step involved reducing the number of multicast subscriptions from the Omni Root model, which had been over-subscribed to 14 ST models. This was accomplished by auditing and cleaning up unnecessary multicast listeners, ensuring only essential models remained subscribed.
The remediation was executed through the deployment of D99462463 (https://www.internalfb.com/diff/D99462463), which updated the sitevar configuration for MRS_DELTA_UPDATE_SUBSCRIBER to decrease the number of multicast listeners. This change directly mitigated the resource contention, allowing the training QPS to recover as confirmed in system logs and workchat updates. Additionally, a follow-up diff, D99470486 (https://www.internalfb.com/diff/D99470486), was reviewed and accepted to further remove unnecessary models and correct the subscription list, ensuring only required models were maintained.
No further remediation improvements are currently planned or documented in the SEV manager or related tasks. The immediate actions taken have successfully restored online training QPS and resolved the incident.

## Prevention
To prevent recurrence of the IG Reels retrieval Omni Root model online training QPS drop, we have implemented targeted configuration changes and established new operational safeguards. The primary prevention action was the reduction of multicast subscriptions for the production Omni model, which was achieved through the deployment of D99462463 (https://www.internalfb.com/diff/D99462463). This diff updated the MRS_DELTA_UPDATE_SUBSCRIBER sitevar, limiting the number of subscriber models and directly mitigating resource contention that previously blocked training.
Additionally, unnecessary models were removed from the multicast list via D99470486 (https://www.internalfb.com/diff/D99470486), ensuring only essential listeners remain. Going forward, the team has agreed to involve relevant stakeholders in reviewing and stamping multicast sitevar changes, as discussed in the SEV workchat. This collaborative review process will help prevent unchecked growth in multicast subscriptions during model role transitions and ensure configuration hygiene.
These actions are now part of our operational workflow, and all future multicast subscription changes will be audited and approved by the team to maintain system stability and prevent similar incidents.
